1 What a pose is — the 60-second mental model

A pose is a named position you can send the arm back to: park, start, pick, handoff. It is not six numbers.

Two positions can both be perfectly safe while the straight line between them drives the claw through the bench, the wire loom, or the base housing. So a pose is the route you drove, in the order you drove it — the console calls each stop along the way a step.

The steps come from the angles the arm was actually commanded to, at each point where you stopped and looked at it. Nothing is guessed and nothing is interpolated. That is why letting go is the moment a step is taken: letting go is when you stopped and looked.

A pose saved with no steps records only where the arm ended up. The library shows it as REFERENCE ONLY and refuses to load it, because there is no safe way to drive somewhere when nobody recorded how you got there.

One extra step is taken for you: the position the arm is standing in when you first touch the jog bar. Without it, playback would jump straight to step 1 from wherever the arm happens to be — every joint moving at once — which is the exact hazard above.

2 Phase 0 — everything below runs with servo power OFF

Nothing in this phase moves the arm. The console is built to be exercised end to end with zero servos powered: the board answers, the controls respond, the firmware acknowledges.

P0.1  Run the preflight from the repository root:

powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File Documentation\bench-preflight.ps1
8 passed, 0 failed. It confirms the Pi is serving exactly the console in the repo, that the pose file matches, and that the hold daemon is stopped.
If it says the hold daemon is RUNNING, stop hold_arm_pi.py reports EN=1 for joints whose supply rail is dead. Switching servo power on with it running snaps every joint to the setpoint it was last holding. Stop it before the rocker goes anywhere near ON.

P0.2  Double-click Arm Console on the desktop. A black window opens and stays open — that window is the connection to the Pi. The browser follows a few seconds later.

The TEACH tab selected, and six joints listed in the left rail.

P0.3  Click through RUN POSES, CALIBRATION, DIAGNOSTICS, and back to TEACH.

The red E-STOP and the safety line stay on screen on all four tabs. They are outside the tabs on purpose.

P0.4  Press CONNECT.

The status chip turns green and reads Connected. No red notices.

P0.5  Go to CALIBRATION and click J3 Elbow.

The envelope reads 0° and 30°, and the pill beside ENVELOPE says ACKNOWLEDGED.
P0.5 is the gate. Do not pass it. DEFAULT instead of ACKNOWLEDGED means the controller has not confirmed the limits and joint-limits.csv never loaded — every joint is on the firmware's 70–110° placeholder, which is not this arm's travel. Opening the serial port resets the board and the firmware keeps nothing across a reset, so the file is re-read on every connect. If it did not, use LOAD LIMITS FILE on the CALIBRATION tab and check again.

3 Phase 1 — power on, one joint, prove the recording

Hand under the forearm. Then the rocker.

T1  Select J1 Shoulder, press ENABLE, type the angle it is sitting at judged by eye, press CONFIRM.

Nothing jumps. The first pulse is exactly the value you typed, which is the whole point of being asked.

T2  The teach table is empty and reads “No steps yet” under SAVE POSE. Touch the jog bar and hold it still for a second.

A row labelled start appears before the arm has gone anywhere.

T3  Jog about 10°, then let go.

A second row appears, carrying the new angle.

T4  Nudge it barely — a degree or so — and let go.

No new row. Below 2° is a nudge, not a step.

T5  Hold the jog bar so the joint is moving, and while still holding it, click a different joint tile in the left rail.

The joint stops immediately.

T6  Untick Save a step each time I let go. Jog and release: no row. Tick it back on.

4 Phase 2 — make the park pose

Park is where the arm rests with the power off. It is the one pose worth being fussy about.

Rules, not angles No angles are prescribed here on purpose. The elbow's travel is 0–30° now, not 0–66° — the fold that storage recorded does not exist any more, and nobody has looked at the arm's geometry since that re-lock. Judge it with your eyes.
  • Stay at least 3° inside every limit. A joint against its own end has nowhere to back off to. joint-limits.csv does this itself: J1 homes at 1° and not the locked 0°, J3 at 15° and not 30°.
  • Fold over the base rather than reaching out over the bench.
  • Leave the wrist alone unless you are sure of its travel. J4's ends are still unconfirmed, and driving a wrist blind is how the claw gets pushed into the mat.
  • Think about power-off. The forearm swings down through the base area where the loom and the blue cable sit.

T7  Drive there in 8–12° steps, letting go at each one. Watch the arm, not the screen.

T8  Read the line above SAVE POSE.

“Will save N steps as the path to this pose.”
If it still says “No steps yet”, do not save Nothing was recorded, and saving would produce another Parked: a row that looks saved and can never be played. Go back and jog with the tick box on.

T9  Type park in the name box and press SAVE POSE.

A green notice naming the pose and its step count.

T10  Check for an amber warning about a joint on a soft limit.

No amber warning. If you get one you are parked on an end — back off a few degrees and save again. It warns and still saves; parking on a limit deliberately is your call, just not one to make by accident.

T11  Go to RUN POSES.

park shows a green READY badge with its step count, and its LOAD button is enabled.

5 Phase 3 — the first playback

Nothing taught on this arm has ever been played back. Go slowly, and keep a hand free for the rocker.

T12  Jog the arm away from park, somewhere obviously different.

T13  On RUN POSES, press LOAD on park.

You land on TEACH with the recorded steps in the table. Read them before going further — that review is the reason LOAD and PLAY are two buttons.

T14  Press PLAY.

The arm retraces the path step by step and ends at park.
Abort — E-STOP or the rocker — if any of these happen
  • any joint reports CL=1 (clamped) or JTO=1 (joint timeout)
  • the watchdog latches, or a joint detaches on its own
  • anything moves that you did not expect, or in an order you did not expect
  • the claw heads toward the bench, the mat, the loom or the base housing
If it aborts, write down which step number it was on. The step number plus the table is the whole diagnosis.
If T14 is clean, the run has succeeded Phase 4 is optional. Stop here with a working park pose and you have got what this session was for.

Do this before closing anything. The pose is written into arm-poses.csv on the Pi, next to the bridge — which is a deploy copy, not a git checkout. A pose that lives only there is invisible to git and to anyone else working on this arm.
